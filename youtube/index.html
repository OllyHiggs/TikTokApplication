Internal proposal — commerce media

Put licensed YouTube creator content where the sale actually happens: the product page.

A retail media product that licenses YouTube creator videos and places them natively inside merchant e-commerce environments — on product detail pages and on search & category pages.

On product pages, a licensed creator video sits inside the product's own media gallery, presented natively alongside product photography — not as an ad unit — and the creator is paid on sales attributed post-view. On retailer search and category pages, licensed creator content appears natively within the results grid, and the creator is paid on impressions. The content is paid for by the brand or retailer; it is not organic embedding. It extends the closed-loop logic Google is already building through the Commerce Media Suite and YouTube Creator Partnerships to a surface neither currently touches: the retailer's own digital shelf. A second phase extends the same model into physical retail via shelf-edge displays.

Why now

Google built the plumbing this needs — pointed the other way

H1 2026 has been the busiest stretch yet for Google's commerce-media build-out. The Commerce Media Suite added Kroger in March (with SKU-level sales reporting), Best Buy and Shipt the same month, and Costco and Dollar General at Brandcast in May. On 11 June, Walmart Connect data became activatable inside DV360 — starting with YouTube — with closed-loop measurement tying video impressions directly to Walmart sales.

Every one of those deals moves retailer data into Google's buying tools. None of them moves YouTube content onto retailer surfaces. That reverse flow is the white space this proposal fills — the complementary second half of the Commerce Media Suite, not a competitor to it.

YouTube Creator Partnerships (unified from BrandConnect on 24 March 2026) already launched in seven markets including the UK, giving brands creator discovery, deal management, content approvals, rights to amplify creator videos as ads, and unified measurement inside Google Ads and DV360. That licensing spine partly exists — this proposal extends its amplification surface from YouTube itself to retailer inventory. YouTube's Content ID heritage means it is the only platform with industrial-scale rights infrastructure to do this; no other platform has an equivalent.

The model isn't hypothetical, either. The YouTube Shopping affiliate programme already pays creators on retailer-attributed sales when a viewer clicks a tagged product on a YouTube video and buys on the retailer's site. This proposal inverts the direction of travel: instead of the viewer moving from YouTube to the retailer, the licensed content moves to the retailer, with the same commission logic applied at the destination.

The white space is also geographic: every Commerce Media Suite retailer today is US. No UK retailer — Tesco, Sainsbury's Nectar360, Boots, Asda, Ocado, Co-op — has an equivalent YouTube integration.

The mechanic

Two surfaces, two ways to get paid

Product detail page

Paid on attributed sales

The licensed creator video sits inside the product's own media gallery — native to the page design, not a bolted-on ad unit. No YouTube chrome, no end screens, no recommended videos.

The retailer's server reports purchase events back server-to-server, keyed by creator and SKU — the same pattern as Google's Enhanced Conversions, with Ads Data Hub as the clean room. Viewer watches the creator's video on the PDP, buys the SKU, the creator earns a commission on that sale.

Search & category page

Paid on impressions

Licensed creator content appears natively within the results grid — a creator tile sitting among product tiles, not a display banner interrupting them.

Search pages are discovery moments, not purchase moments, so per-creator sales attribution here is noisy. Impression-based pay is the defensible model — which is exactly why it needs agreed viewability standards and MRC-style accreditation before money moves.

In-store: the physical shelf

Phase 2 — roadmap, not the pilot

The same licensed content, extended onto electronic shelf-edge displays — Vusion's in-store screens replace the paper price label with a small display capable of carrying the same creator clip that runs on the PDP.

Small-format, high-frequency stores are the natural fit: the same research-heavy categories that make YouTube content valuable online carry the same value standing at the physical shelf.
